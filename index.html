04 / Capture journeys

Relief at the moment of saving.

A. Mobile · save without breaking focus

1 / ShareChrome on Android → Share → Know. On iOS, use the Share Sheet.
2 / Confirm“Link saved” appears once saving succeeds. Enrichment can still be pending.
3 / ReturnClose the tab or optionally add a short “why I saved this” note.

Manual URL entry offers the same confirmation. Capture availability and first-time setup need separate onboarding review.

What if saving fails or the link already exists?

No success message until the link is recorded. Offline or failed saves clearly say “Not saved yet” with a retry path. For an existing source: “Already saved · open existing item.” Proposed: retain a new discovery moment without duplicating the source.

A failed preview should tell you what to do.

Sign-in / access neededOpen in an authenticated browser, then choose capture. If access is only suspected, say “Access may be restricted.”
Temporary connection issueThe link remains saved. Offer retry; do not label it a paywall.
Site blocks retrievalExplain that automatic reading was blocked. Offer permitted browser capture if available.
Page removed / missingKeep the link, existing preview, and personal notes. Explain what content remains available.
Unsupported / unknownKeep a link-only item. Allow a note or later retry without promising a summary.
Authenticated capture is a deliberate action on the visible page. The experience does not ask users to send browser cookies.

What is certain, and what is a suggestion?

Personal activities are recorded as “You marked this finished.” A source claim says “This page states…” with its origin. A supported relationship includes its supporting source. A speculative connection says “AI suggestion” and explains why. Uncertain matches remain suggestions, never silently becoming established facts.

What happens when processing is paused or incomplete?

The saved item remains browseable as a link. State whether the content is waiting, restricted, unavailable, paused by preference, or limited by quota. A partial preview never pretends to be a complete reading of the page. No unreadable page receives an invented summary.

No unsolicited reminders or notifications are assumed. Saved does not mean read; quizzes based on unread items should ask for that choice explicitly.

08 / Personal data & processing preferences

Memory should feel trustworthy.

Account & ownership

Sign inGoogle sign-in; make the active account visible, especially on shared devices.
Private collectionYour saved content, notes, and activities are personal. No public sharing is implied.
ExportClearly state whether links, captured text, notes, relationships, and history are included.
DeleteDistinguish removing a source, removing personal activity, and deleting the account. Show scope and consequences before confirmation.

Processing choices Conceptual settings

AI assistanceExplain what content may be processed and by whom before the user opts in.
Per-item controlKeep a sensitive item as a link only; skip summaries and suggestions.
Usage visibilityShow quota and any spending limit. If exhausted: “Saved · AI enrichment paused.”
Change preferencesExplain the effect on new processing and previously generated material. Do not imply an opt-out automatically erases old results.
Open UX gap: deletion and export need understandable scope, progress, and completion states. Retention and third-party handling must be explained before making privacy promises. No legal compliance claim is made by this presentation.

What feels right?
What needs another pass?

Confirmed from your brief

The foundations

Save immediately. Enrich later. Separate sources from entities, activities from facts, and discovery from consumption. Keep evidence visible. Phase 1 only.

Proposals · Not approved

The experience

Inbox means unreviewed. Five peer navigation views. Focused graph neighborhoods. Explicit entity-match correction. Optional capture note. Insights stay on demand.

Open / No rejected choices yet

The boundaries

Capture guarantees, home view, organization workload, entity ambiguity, session grouping, TV progress, and deletion scope need product decisions.

Three decisions with the biggest UX impact

1. What must “saved” guarantee?

Recommendation for review: confirm the link immediately and show content preservation separately. A removed page can leave only a URL unless content was successfully captured. Should capturing a durable reading copy be central to the promise?

2. Where should returning users land?

Inbox supports capture and triage; a chronological home supports memory; a library supports reading and watching. Recommendation for review: begin with the inbox, with one-click routes to history and library. Avoid turning the home into an obligation to clear a backlog.

3. How much organization should require attention?

Recommendation for review: topics can remain suggestions without blocking use. Ambiguous entity merges deserve visible review and a correction path. Decide whether users approve every match or only uncertain ones.

Discover → save with a clear receipt → optionally complete restricted capture → understand suggested organization → return through inbox, history, library, graph, or search → record an activity or request a grounded insight.
